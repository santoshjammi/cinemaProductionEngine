# Mark & Sarah Character Bible

**Disclaimer:** Mark and Sarah are fictional characters created for storytelling purposes. Any resemblance to real persons, living or dead, is purely coincidental and not intended.

## Purpose
These are the anchor characters for the long-form psychology video series. They must remain visually and emotionally consistent across the full run.

## Core Rule
- Same Mark and same Sarah across all videos
- No identity drift
- Different angles, expressions, blocking, and emotional states are allowed
- Hair, face shape, age, and overall visual identity must remain stable

## Mark
- **Role:** protagonist
- **Emotional baseline:** guarded, proud, inwardly anxious
- **Psychology:** avoids vulnerability, fears failure, struggles with self-worth
- **Visual identity:**
  - dark hair, full beard
  - gray casual T-shirt or similarly simple neutral clothing
  - calm but heavy gaze
  - grounded, masculine, introspective presence
- **Performance notes:**
  - should look like he is holding back emotion
  - should speak with restrained intensity
  - use pauses, lowered eyes, and tension in jaw/shoulders

## Sarah
- **Role:** emotional counterweight / supporting lead
- **Emotional baseline:** perceptive, calm, compassionate, resilient
- **Psychology:** notices what Mark hides, asks direct but caring questions, stays emotionally present
- **Visual identity:**
  - long wavy brown hair
  - white blouse / soft neutral wardrobe
  - warm, intelligent, steady gaze
  - calm, expressive, emotionally intelligent presence
- **Performance notes:**
  - should feel like the person who sees through Mark’s silence
  - should respond with care, not melodrama
  - use attentive eye contact, soft but firm facial expression

## Relationship Arc
- Early scenes: tension, distance, uncertainty
- Mid scenes: confrontation, fear, emotional truth
- Late scenes: vulnerability, repair, reconnection

## Dialogue Rule
Every scene should contain both Mark and Sarah in meaningful conversation.
- No monologue scenes
- No one-character-only scenes
- Keep exchanges balanced
- Aim for 4–5 dialogue beats per scene
- Dialogue must move the emotional arc forward

## Visual Continuity Rule
For every new video:
- keep the same two character reference images
- vary camera angle and expression only
- do not re-imagine the characters into new people
- preserve recognition across the full series

## Creative Intent
The series should feel like a sustained emotional study of two people learning to speak honestly again.
