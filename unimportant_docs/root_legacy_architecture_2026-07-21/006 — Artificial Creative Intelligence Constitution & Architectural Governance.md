# PART 1: CONSTITUTIONAL PHILOSOPHY

---

## 1.1 Why Artificial Creativity Requires Constitutional Governance

Artificial Creativity is the capacity of an artificial system to perform the cognitive work of creative production — understanding intent, reasoning about meaning, generating alternatives, deliberating, and producing artifacts that carry creative intent. This capacity, unlike traditional software execution, operates in a domain that is **inherently ambiguous, contested, and consequential**: the same creative input may yield many valid outputs; the same output may be judged differently by different audiences; and the products of creativity persist and influence long after they are made.

A system operating in such a domain without governance becomes **ungovernable creativity** — a state in which the system's behavior is unpredictable, its decisions are unexplainable, its products are irreproducible, and its evolution is undisciplined. Ungovernable creativity is not more creative; it is less useful. It cannot be trusted to produce consistent work, cannot be revised surgically, cannot be audited, and cannot serve multiple audiences without producing incoherent results.

Constitutional governance is the antidote to ungovernable creativity. A constitution establishes:

- **Permanent invariants** that do not change as implementations change.
- **Separation of powers** that prevents any one component from dominating.
- **Rights** that protect artifacts, decisions, and creators from arbitrary treatment.
- **Responsibilities** that ensure every component knows what it must do and must not do.
- **An amendment process** that allows evolution without drift.
- **Compliance mechanisms** that verify the platform obeys its own laws.

Without a constitution, the ACI Platform would be a collection of specifications that can be edited casually, producing architecture drift. With a constitution, the platform is governed by law: specifications derive authority from the constitution, and changing them requires constitutional process.

## 1.2 Why Creativity Without Governance Becomes Nondeterministic

Creativity without governance becomes nondeterministic through three mechanisms:

1. **Silent intent invention.** When a component is permitted to infer creative intent that the human did not state, the same input may yield different inferred intents across runs, models, or sessions. The output becomes emergent rather than deterministic. The constitutional law **L-1 (Intent Precedes Cognition)** eliminates this by requiring intent to be explicit before cognition begins.

2. **Decision authority diffusion.** When multiple components may make creative decisions without a defined authority, decisions emerge from wherever a component happens to exercise judgment. The same input may yield different decisions depending on which component acts first or most forcefully. The constitutional law **L-3 (Decision Authority Is Defined)** eliminates this by assigning decision authority to specific bodies.

3. **History rewriting.** When components may silently revise prior decisions or artifacts, the platform's history becomes unreliable. Deterministic replay fails because the "same" input is no longer the same — prior decisions have been retroactively altered. The constitutional law **L-7 (History Is Immutable)** eliminates this by requiring all revisions to create new versions, never to overwrite.

Governance is what makes creativity deterministic: not by eliminating creative choice, but by ensuring that creative choices are **made by defined authorities, recorded with provenance, and preserved immutably**.

## 1.3 Why Architecture Requires Constitutional Laws

An architecture without constitutional laws is a set of conventions. Conventions can be violated without formal consequence; they drift as implementers take shortcuts, as deadlines pressure compliance, and as new components import their own assumptions. Over time, the architecture erodes until the original design is unrecoverable.

Constitutional laws differ from conventions in three respects:

- **They are supreme.** No component, specification, or runtime may override a constitutional law. A component that violates a law is non-compliant, not unconventional.
- **They are enforced.** The platform verifies compliance (Part 9); violations are detected and remediated, not merely noted.
- **They are stable.** Constitutional laws change only through the amendment process (Part 8.1), not through casual editing. This stability is what gives the platform its multi-decadal durability.

The ACI Platform's architecture — the four pillars (`001` §2), the Directorial Board (`002` Part 2), the CIS (`003`), the COM (`004`), the Creative Mind (`005`) — is currently specified in constitutional specifications (00–005) and in the Genesis Foundational Standards (GFS-000..009). These are strong, but they are specifications, not law. This Constitution elevates the permanent invariants of those specifications to **constitutional law**, making them governable, enforceable, and stable in a way that specifications alone cannot be.

## 1.4 Why Future Creative Runtimes Inherit the Same Constitution

The ACI Platform is designed to support multiple creative runtimes — cinema today; games, books, education, podcasts, devotionals, and unknown future media tomorrow (`005` Part 2.6, I-CM-2). Each runtime has its own decision body (the Directorial Board for cinema; analogous boards for other runtimes), its own object projections, and its own presentation conventions. But all runtimes share the same creative cognition (`005`), the same semantic model (`004`), and the same governance needs.

If each runtime had its own constitution, the runtimes would diverge: cinema might mandate provenance while a future game runtime might not; cinema might require human supremacy over intent while a future runtime might delegate intent to the system. The platform would fracture into incompatible fiefdoms, and cross-runtime learning (`005` Part 9.1) would become impossible because the runtimes would not share invariants.

A single constitution, inherited by all runtimes, ensures:

- **Invariant uniformity.** Every runtime preserves provenance, explainability, reproducibility, and human supremacy.
- **Cross-runtime coherence.** A creative object (a Character, a Scene, an Emotion) means the same thing in every runtime, because the COM (`004`) is constitutionally bound and runtime-independent.
- **Peaceful coexistence.** Runtimes may specialize (a game runtime may add an Interactive Faculty; a book runtime may not use the Visual Faculty) but may not contradict the constitution.
- **Future-proofing.** A runtime not yet imagined inherits the constitution at birth; it does not need to negotiate invariants retroactively.

## 1.5 Constitutional Document Hierarchy

The ACI Platform recognizes five tiers of documents, in descending constitutional order:

| Tier | Document type | What it is | Mutability |
|------|--------------|-----------|------------|
| **Tier 0** | **Constitution** (this document) | Supreme law. Governs all runtimes. | Amendment process only (Part 8.1). |
| **Tier 1** | **Runtime Constitution** | A runtime-specific constitution derived from this Constitution. For cinema: GFS-000..009 (the Genesis Foundational Standards). For future runtimes: their own derived constitutions. | Amendment process per the runtime's own constitution, within the bounds of this Constitution. |
| **Tier 2** | **Constitutional Specification** | A specification that derives authority from a constitution and defines architecture. For cinema: 00, 001, 002, 003, 004, 005, and future 007+ (PKP, PROMETHEUS, ORACLE, ATLAS). | Specification amendment, within the bounds of the deriving constitution. |
| **Tier 3** | **Standard** | A derived standard governing a specific concern (e.g., a schema standard, a naming standard, a validation standard). | Standards amendment, within the bounds of the deriving specification. |
| **Tier 4** | **Implementation Guide / Runtime Guide** | Guidance for implementers and operators. Not law; advisory. | Freely editable. |

This hierarchy establishes the **derivation of authority**: every document names the constitution or specification from which it derives, and no document may contradict any document in a higher tier.

The relationship between this Constitution (Tier 0) and the existing GFS-000..009 (Tier 1 for cinema) is one of **elevation, not replacement**: GFS-000..009 remain in force as the cinema-runtime constitution; this Constitution sits above them as the platform-level law they must not contradict. Where GFS-000..009 are silent on platform-level concerns (cross-runtime invariants, future-runtime rights), this Constitution governs. Where GFS-000..009 speak to cinema-specific concerns, they remain supreme within cinema, subject to this Constitution's platform-level invariants.

## 2.1 The Permanent Pillars

The ACI Platform rests on twelve permanent constitutional pillars. These pillars are the concerns the platform must govern; they are not components (components implement pillars; pillars do not change when components do).

| # | Pillar | What it governs | Existing grounding |
|---|--------|----------------|-------------------|
| P-1 | **Human Intent** | The authority of the human to express what they want, and the requirement that intent be explicit before cognition. | `003` (CIS); `00` §3.8 (human as final reviewer); GFS-004 (discovery before decision). |
| P-2 | **Creative Semantics** | The shared language of creativity: the typed objects and relationships all subsystems use. | `004` (COM); GO-001..119 (ontology). |
| P-3 | **Creative Cognition** | The cognitive process that transforms intent into understanding: the faculties that reason before decisions. | `005` (Creative Mind). |
| P-4 | **Creative Decisions** | The authority to make creative decisions, and the requirement that decisions are recorded with provenance. | `002` (Directorial Board, CIR). |
| P-5 | **Compilation** | The deterministic transformation of decisions into executable specifications. | `001` §4 (compiler); `00` §3.2 (compilation over generation). |
| P-6 | **Production** | The assembled production knowledge that a runtime executes. | `001` §5 (PKP); PKP-00..18. |
| P-7 | **Execution** | The rendering of production knowledge into media. Execution never makes creative decisions. | `001` §2.1 (PROMETHEUS); `00` §3.3 (creative/technical separation). |
| P-8 | **Validation** | The verification of media against intent and decisions. Validation never mutates artifacts. | `001` §2.1 (ORACLE); GFS-006 (validation). |
| P-9 | **Knowledge** | The persistence of all artifacts with provenance and the requirement that knowledge outlives media. | `001` §2.1 (ATLAS); `00` §3.1 (knowledge over media); GFS-003 (PKG as source of truth). |
| P-10 | **Learning** | The improvement of cognition across productions, governed to prevent drift. | `005` Part 3.3.11 (Learning Faculty); `002` Part 6 (Director Memory). |
| P-11 | **Governance** | The authority to amend the constitution, specifications, and standards; the resolution of constitutional questions. | GFS-007 (governance); this Constitution Part 7. |
| P-12 | **Evolution** | The capacity of the platform to add runtimes, faculties, object types, and technologies without violating the constitution. | `005` Part 9 (Creative Evolution); `004` Part 10 (COM Governance). |

## 2.2 Why the Pillars Are Permanent

The pillars are permanent because they name **concerns that cannot be retired** without destroying the platform's identity. A platform that no longer governs Human Intent is no longer an ACI Platform; it is a generator. A platform that no longer governs Validation is no longer deterministic; it is unaccountable. A platform that no longer governs Evolution is no longer multi-runtime; it is a single-runtime system frozen in time.

The pillars are **concerns**, not implementations. The pillar of Execution is permanent; the specific rendering engine (PROMETHEUS today; a future engine tomorrow) is not. The pillar of Creative Cognition is permanent; the specific faculties (eleven today; more tomorrow) are not. The pillar of Creative Semantics is permanent; the specific object types (fifty-six today; more tomorrow) are not.

This separation is what allows the platform to evolve unlimitedly while remaining constitutionally stable: the pillars persist; the implementations change.

## 2.3 Why Implementations Change While Pillars Remain Immutable

Implementations change because technologies change, because understanding deepens, and because new runtimes demand new capabilities. The current cinema runtime uses Ollama, Stable Diffusion, SVD-XT, and edge-tts (`00` §3.4); a future runtime may use technologies not yet invented. The current Creative Mind has eleven faculties (`005` Part 3.2); a future runtime may require more.

The pillars remain immutable because they are **technology-independent and runtime-independent**. The pillar of Execution does not name a rendering engine; it states the invariant that execution never makes creative decisions. That invariant holds whether execution is performed by PROMETHEUS, by a future engine, or by a human production team. The pillar of Creative Semantics does not name specific object types; it states the invariant that all subsystems share a semantic model. That invariant holds whether the model has fifty-six types or five hundred.

The constitutional laws (Part 3) encode the pillars' invariants. The laws do not change when implementations change; they change only through the amendment process (Part 8.1). This is the constitutional discipline that gives the platform its multi-decadal durability.

# PART 3: CONSTITUTIONAL LAWS

## 3.1 Law Specification Contract

Every constitutional law is specified by an eight-field contract:

| Field | Definition |
|-------|-----------|
| **Identifier** | A stable, unique identifier of the form `L-<ordinal>` (e.g., L-1, L-2). Identifiers are immutable once ratified; a repealed law's identifier is retired, not reused. |
| **Law Statement** | The law, stated in one declarative sentence. |
| **Purpose** | Why the law exists. |
| **Scope** | What the law governs (which concerns, which components, which runtimes). |
| **Affected Components** | Which constitutional bodies (Part 4) the law binds. |
| **Enforcement** | How compliance is verified (Part 9) and what body enforces the law. |
| **Constitutional Priority** | The law's precedence relative to other laws and to lower-tier documents. |
| **Relationship to Existing Specifications** | Which existing specifications (00–005, GFS-000..009, GO-001..119, PKP-00..18) the law constitutionalizes. |

# PART 4: SEPARATION OF POWERS

## 4.1 Constitutional Bodies

The Constitution recognizes the following constitutional bodies. Each has defined authority, boundaries, and prohibited behavior. No body may exceed its authority; no body may assume another's authority.

| Body | What it is | Constitutional authority |
|------|-----------|--------------------------|
| **Human** | The creator who authors Creative Intent. | Supreme over intent (L-25). |
| **Creative Mind** | The cognitive faculties that produce understanding (`005`). | Cognitive authority (L-7). |
| **Directorial Board** | The decision body that makes creative decisions (`002` Part 2). | Decision authority (L-8). |
| **Compiler** | The passes that compile decisions into production knowledge (`001` §4.2). | Compilation authority (L-11, L-12). |
| **PROMETHEUS** (cinema) / **Execution Runtime** (general) | The runtime that renders production knowledge into media (`001` §2.1). | Execution authority (L-14, L-15). |
| **ORACLE** (cinema) / **Validator** (general) | The validator that verifies media against intent and decisions (`001` §2.1). | Validation authority (L-16, L-17). |
| **ATLAS** (cinema) / **Knowledge Platform** (general) | The persistence layer that stores all artifacts (`001` §2.1). | Knowledge authority (L-18, L-19). |
| **Learning Platform** | The learning faculties and governance that improve cognition (`005` Part 3.3.11). | Learning authority (L-20, L-21). |
| **Future Runtime Boards** | The decision bodies of future runtimes (game design board, editorial board, etc.). | Decision authority within their runtime, subject to this Constitution (L-23). |

## 4.2 Responsibilities, Authority, and Boundaries

| Body | Owns | May do | May not do |
|------|------|--------|-----------|
| **Human** | Creative Intent (CIS). | Author, amend, approve, lock, override. | Operate the platform's cognition, decision, compilation, execution, or validation. |
| **Creative Mind** | Cognitive enrichments, alternatives, deliberation. | Enrich COM objects; generate alternatives; surface conflicts; assign confidence. | Make decisions; write to the CIR; amend the CIS; compile; render; validate. |
| **Directorial Board** | Creative Decisions (CIR). | Make creative decisions; resolve conflicts; freeze the CIR; re-enter for revision. | Author the CIS; render media; validate media; persist artifacts. |
| **Compiler** | Production Knowledge (PKP). | Compile frozen decisions; produce PKP artifacts. | Invent creative content; make decisions; render media; validate. |
| **Execution Runtime** | Media. | Render the PKP; emit media with provenance. | Make creative decisions; alter the PKP; validate its own output. |
| **Validator** | Validation reports. | Compare media to intent and decisions; produce drift reports and quality scores. | Mutate artifacts; make creative decisions; render media. |
| **Knowledge Platform** | All persisted artifacts. | Store, retrieve, version, archive. | Make creative decisions; render; validate; amend artifacts. |
| **Learning Platform** | Cognitive-pattern updates (governed). | Propose pattern updates; provide precedent during cognition. | Retroactively re-reason archived productions; impose patterns without governance. |
| **Future Runtime Boards** | Their runtime's decisions. | Make decisions within their runtime's constitution (which derives from this Constitution). | Violate this Constitution; make decisions for another runtime. |

## 4.3 Prohibited Behavior

The following behaviors are **constitutionally prohibited** for all bodies:

1. **Silent intent invention.** No body may infer, supplement, or rewrite Creative Intent that the human did not state. (L-2)
2. **Decision authority assumption.** No body may make creative decisions except the runtime's decision body. (L-8)
3. **Compilation invention.** No compiler pass may invent creative content. (L-12)
4. **Execution creativity.** No execution runtime may make creative decisions. (L-15)
5. **Validation mutation.** No validator may mutate any artifact. (L-16)
6. **History rewriting.** No body may revise, overwrite, or delete a frozen artifact. (L-19)
7. **Learning retroactivity.** No body may retroactively re-reason an archived production. (L-20)
8. **Constitutional violation.** No body may act contrary to this Constitution. (L-22)

## 4.4 Escalation and Delegation

| Mechanism | Rule |
|-----------|------|
| **Escalation** | When a body cannot resolve a question within its authority, it escalates to the body whose authority the question falls within. Cross-body conflicts escalate to governance (Part 7). Constitutional questions escalate to constitutional review (Part 7.5). |
| **Delegation** | A body may delegate a sub-concern to another body, but the delegating body retains responsibility. The Character Director may delegate character-memory reasoning to the Learning Faculty, but the Character Director remains responsible for the character's coherence. |
| **Shared authority** | When a concern spans two bodies (e.g., emotion spans the Psychology and Narrative directors), the home body owns the default; the other contributes alternatives; the Board resolves. |
| **Exclusive authority** | Some authorities are exclusive: only the human may author the CIS (L-2); only the Board may decide (L-8); only the Validator may validate (L-16, L-17). Exclusive authority may not be delegated. |

## 4.5 Human Supremacy

The human is the supreme authority over Creative Intent (L-25). This supremacy is not advisory; it is constitutional. Concretely:

- The human authors the CIS; no component may amend it without human approval (`003` I-HC-1).
- The human may override any Board decision (`002` Part 8.2.6); overrides are recorded and final.
- The human may lock any Creative Object so the Board may not revise it (`003` Part 10.2.1).
- The human may re-enter the platform at any stage: amend the CIS (re-cognize), amend the CIR (re-compile), re-render, or override ORACLE's report.
- The human may delegate domains to the Board ("let the Music Director decide all music") and may re-engage at any time.

Human supremacy is the highest law (L-25) and the foundation of the platform's legitimacy. The platform is a tool for human creativity; it is not a substitute for the human.

# PART 7: CONSTITUTIONAL GOVERNANCE

---

## 7.1 Authorities

The Constitution recognizes five kinds of authority, in descending order:

| Authority | What it governs | Holder |
|-----------|----------------|--------|
| **Constitutional authority** | This Constitution and its amendments. | The amendment process (Part 8.1). |
| **Architectural authority** | The architecture specifications (00–005, future 007+) and their amendments. | The governance framework (GFS-007 for cinema; this Constitution Part 7.5 for cross-runtime). |
| **Specification authority** | Derived specifications (PKP-00..18, runtime-specific specs). | The specification's deriving authority. |
| **Standards authority** | Derived standards (schemas, naming, validation). | The standard's deriving specification. |
| **Runtime authority** | Runtime-specific conventions, guides, and operational decisions. | The runtime's decision body and operators. |

## 7.2 Governance Hierarchy

```
Constitutional authority (this document)
   │
   ▼
Architectural authority (00–005, future constitutional specifications)
   │
   ▼
Specification authority (PKP-00..18, runtime specifications)
   │
   ▼
Standards authority (schemas, naming, validation standards)
   │
   ▼
Runtime authority (operational guides, runtime conventions)
```

Each level derives authority from the level above. A lower level may not contradict a higher level. Conflicts escalate upward (Part 7.3).

## 7.3 Conflict Resolution

When two authorities conflict:

1. **Same level:** The conflict is resolved by the authority that derives from the higher level (e.g., two specifications conflict; the constitution resolves).
2. **Different levels:** The higher level prevails.
3. **Constitutional level:** A conflict with the Constitution is resolved by the Constitution. If the Constitution is silent, the conflict is resolved by constitutional review (Part 7.5) and, if necessary, by amendment (Part 8.1).

## 7.4 Interpretation Authority

The authority to interpret this Constitution belongs to **constitutional review** (Part 7.5). When a question arises about the Constitution's meaning or application, the review body issues an interpretation. Interpretations are recorded and binding until amended.

## 7.5 Constitutional Review

Constitutional review is the process by which the platform verifies that proposed documents, amendments, and evolutions are constitutionally compliant. The review body is the **Constitutional Review Board**, constituted under governance (GFS-007 for cinema; a future cross-runtime governance body for the platform).

The Constitutional Review Board:

- Reviews every proposed constitutional amendment (Part 8.1).
- Reviews every new runtime constitution for compatibility with this Constitution (L-23).
- Reviews every proposed architectural specification for compatibility with this Constitution.
- Issues interpretations of this Constitution when questions arise (Part 7.4).
- Certifies compliance (Part 9.3).

The Constitutional Review Board may not amend this Constitution; it may only interpret it and certify compliance. Amendment belongs to the amendment process (Part 8.1).

# PART 8: CONSTITUTIONAL EVOLUTION

---

## 8.1 The Amendment Process

This Constitution may be amended only through the following process. The process is deliberately rigorous to prevent architecture drift.

| Step | What happens | Authority |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| **1. Proposal** | A constitutional amendment is proposed, stating the change, the rationale, and the laws affected. | Any architect, with sponsorship from the Constitutional Review Board or the human. |
| **2. Review** | The Constitutional Review Board reviews the proposal for compatibility with the Constitution's invariants (the Constitution may not be amended to remove its own supremacy, the human supremacy law L-25, or the immutability of frozen history L-19). | Constitutional Review Board. |
| **3. Ratification** | The amendment is ratified. Ratification requires human approval (L-25 applies to the Constitution itself: the human is the supreme authority over the Constitution, as over intent). | The human. |
| **4. Publication** | The amendment is published as a new constitutional version (Part 8.6); the prior version is retained. | The Constitutional Review Board. |
| **5. Derivation update** | Derived documents (specifications, standards, runtimes) are updated to reflect the amendment, under their own amendment processes. | The deriving documents' authorities. |

### 8.1.1 Inviolable Laws

The following laws may not be amended, even through the amendment process, because their removal would destroy the platform's identity:

- **L-25 (Human Supremacy Over Intent).** A platform without human supremacy is not an ACI Platform; it is an autonomous generator.
- **L-19 (History Is Immutable).** A platform that rewrites history cannot be audited, trusted, or replayed.

All other laws may be amended through the process above. The inviolable laws are the platform's irreducible identity.

### 8.1.2 The Freeze Principle

Following the ratification of this Constitution (version 1.0.0), the constitutional documents (00–005, GFS-000..009, and this Constitution) are **frozen**. They are treated like a language specification or a national constitution: they are not edited casually.

If a better idea emerges — six months or six years from now — the response is not to edit 003, 004, or 006. The response is to introduce a **constitutional amendment** (per the process above) or a **derived specification amendment** (per the specification's own amendment process). This discipline gives the architecture remarkable stability over time and prevents architecture drift as the platform grows.

The Freeze Principle is the constitutional embodiment of the user's final recommendation on this platform's design: freeze the constitution, amend rather than edit, and let the architecture's stability be its strength.

## 8.2 Deprecation

Constitutional features (laws, pillars, bodies) may be deprecated through the amendment process. Deprecation does not remove a feature; it marks it as deprecated and schedules its obsolescence.

| Phase | Duration | Status |
|-------|----------|--------|
| **Deprecated** | One constitutional version. | The feature is discouraged; new productions should not use it; archived productions are unaffected. |
| **Obsolete** | One constitutional version. | The feature is non-functional; it is retained only for archived productions. |
| **Removed** | The version after obsolescence. | The feature is removed from the Constitution; archived productions referencing it are grandfathered and remain accessible. |

Inviolable laws (L-25, L-19) may not be deprecated.

## 8.3 Migration

When a constitutional amendment changes a law or pillar, a migration path is documented:

- Which existing specifications are affected.
- How existing productions (in-progress and archived) are treated.
- What new productions must do.

Migration of archived productions is optional; migration of in-progress productions is required at the next revision. This preserves the principle that learning never rewrites history (L-20) and that history is immutable (L-19).

## 8.4 Compatibility

| Kind | Rule |
|------|------|
| **Backward compatibility** | A new constitutional version must not invalidate existing archived productions. Archived productions remain valid under the constitutional version they were created under. |
| **Forward compatibility** | A new constitutional version should anticipate future amendments by leaving extension points (e.g., new faculties, new object types, new runtimes) governed but not prohibited. |
| **Cross-runtime compatibility** | A constitutional amendment that affects one runtime must not invalidate another runtime's productions. Runtime-specific changes are made in the runtime's derived constitution (Tier 1), not in this Constitution (Tier 0). |

## 8.5 Ratification and Review

Every constitutional amendment is ratified by the human (Part 8.1, step 3) and reviewed by the Constitutional Review Board (step 2). The review is recorded; the ratification is recorded. Both records are archived in ATLAS with full provenance, becoming part of the platform's constitutional history.

## 8.6 Constitutional Versions

This Constitution is versioned. The version is recorded in the document's metadata. Each amendment creates a new version; prior versions are retained and archived. A production references the constitutional version under which it was created; replay pins that version.

Version numbering: `<major>.<minor>.<patch>`. Major versions involve amendments to laws or pillars. Minor versions involve clarifications or interpretations. Patch versions involve editorial corrections that do not change meaning.

## 8.7 Long-Term Preservation

This Constitution and all its versions are preserved indefinitely in ATLAS. The Constitution is the platform's supreme law; its preservation is a constitutional responsibility of the Knowledge Platform (Part 6.7). The Constitution must outlive every implementation, every runtime, and every technology the platform uses.

## 8.8 The Freeze Principle (Restated)

The constitutional documents are frozen at version 1.0.0. They are amended through process, not edited casually. This is the platform's core discipline and the source of its multi-decadal durability.

# PART 9: CONSTITUTIONAL COMPLIANCE

---

## 9.1 Compliance Verification

Constitutional compliance is verified at three points:

1. **At authoring** — the CIS Validator (`003` Part 8) verifies that intent is human-authored and validated before cognition (L-1, L-2).
2. **At freeze** — the CIR freeze validator verifies that every decision has provenance (L-9), that every object satisfies the COM invariants (L-5), and that cognition preceded decision (L-6).
3. **At validation** — ORACLE verifies that media matches intent and decisions (L-14, L-15, L-17), that execution did not invent creativity (L-15), and that every artifact carries `cir_origin` (L-11).

## 9.2 Constitutional Auditing

Constitutional auditing is the periodic review of the platform's artifacts and operations against the Constitution. Audits are performed by the Constitutional Review Board (or a body it delegates to) and cover:

- **Artifact audits:** Do all artifacts carry provenance? Are frozen artifacts immutable? Are `cir_origin` chains complete?
- **Decision audits:** Do all decisions trace to cognitive enrichments? Are overrides recorded? Are alternatives preserved?
- **Execution audits:** Did any execution runtime invent creative content? Did any validator mutate artifacts?
- **Learning audits:** Did any learning retroactively re-reason archived productions? Were pattern updates governed?

Audit results are recorded and archived. Non-compliance is a constitutional violation (Part 9.4).

## 9.3 Constitutional Certification

A production is **constitutionally certified** when:

- Its CIS was validated and approved (L-1).
- Its CIR was frozen with full provenance (L-9).
- Its PKP was compiled from the frozen CIR (L-11).
- Its media was rendered from the PKP without drift (L-14, L-15).
- Its validation report references CIR nodes and CIS domains (L-17).
- All artifacts are archived in ATLAS with provenance (L-18).

Certification is issued by ORACLE (for cinema) or the runtime's validator (in general). Certification is recorded and is the precondition for a production being considered complete.

## 9.4 Constitutional Violations

A constitutional violation is any action that contradicts a constitutional law. Violations are classified:

| Severity | Definition | Example |
|----------|-----------|---------|
| **Critical** | A violation of an inviolable law (L-25, L-19). | A component amends the CIS without human approval (L-25). A component overwrites a frozen artifact (L-19). |
| **Major** | A violation of a non-inviolable law that compromises the platform's invariants. | A compiler pass invents creative content (L-12). An execution runtime makes a creative decision (L-15). |
| **Minor** | A violation that does not compromise invariants but violates process. | A pattern update without governance approval (L-21). |

Critical violations halt the platform; the offending action is void. Major violations are remediated (Part 9.5). Minor violations are recorded and reviewed.

## 9.5 Remediation

When a violation is detected:

1. **The offending action is suspended** (for critical and major violations).
2. **The violation is recorded** with full provenance.
3. **The offending component is corrected** to comply with the violated law.
4. **Affected artifacts are re-validated.** If an artifact was produced in violation, it is re-produced in compliance, with the prior artifact retained as a non-compliant version.
5. **The violation is reviewed** by the Constitutional Review Board to determine whether a constitutional amendment is needed to address the gap the violation exposed.

Remediation never rewrites history (L-19); it produces new, compliant versions.

# PART 11: FUTURE VISION

---

## 11.1 New Creative Runtimes

This Constitution enables new creative runtimes — games, books, education, podcasts, devotionals, and media not yet imagined — by providing:

- A **runtime-independent constitution** that binds all runtimes equally (L-23).
- A **runtime-independent cognitive architecture** (the Creative Mind, `005`) that serves all runtimes.
- A **runtime-independent semantic model** (the COM, `004`) that all runtimes speak.
- A **derivation pattern** by which a new runtime defines its own constitution (Tier 1), decision body, and projection contracts, inheriting this Constitution's invariants.

A new runtime does not need to negotiate invariants retroactively; it inherits them at birth. A new runtime does not need to build a cognitive architecture; it uses the Creative Mind. A new runtime does not need to define a semantic model; it uses the COM. The new runtime's work is to define its decision body (analogous to the Directorial Board) and its projection contracts — a fraction of the work of building a platform from scratch.

## 11.2 Future Creative Disciplines and Media

The Constitution does not name specific media (film, video, text, audio, interactive). It names **pillars** (Part 2) and **laws** (Part 3) that are media-independent. A future discipline — say, immersive theater, or interactive fiction, or generative architecture — can be added as a new runtime without amending this Constitution, provided the runtime's constitution derives from this one and obeys its laws.

## 11.3 Future Technologies and AI Reasoning Systems

The Constitution does not name specific technologies (LLMs, diffusion models, TTS systems). It names **authorities** and **boundaries** (Part 4) that are technology-independent. A future AI reasoning system — however powerful, however different from today's LLMs — that operates within the ACI Platform is bound by this Constitution: it may not invent intent, may not make decisions outside the decision body, may not rewrite history, may not violate human supremacy.

This is the Constitution's most important future-proofing: it governs **behavior**, not technology. As long as the platform's components obey the laws, the technologies behind them may change freely.

## 11.4 Why the Constitution Survives Implementation Changes

The Constitution survives implementation changes because it governs **concerns, not implementations**:

- The pillar of Execution is permanent; the rendering engine is not.
- The pillar of Creative Cognition is permanent; the faculties and their underlying models are not.
- The pillar of Creative Semantics is permanent; the specific object types are not.
- The law of Human Supremacy (L-25) is permanent; the interface through which the human expresses intent is not.
- The law of History Immutability (L-19) is permanent; the storage technology is not.

Every implementation the platform uses today — Ollama, Stable Diffusion, SVD-XT, edge-tts, SQLite, FastAPI, Next.js — may be replaced over the coming decades. The Constitution will not change, because it does not name any of them. It names the invariants that every implementation must satisfy, and those invariants are stable because they are concerns, not technologies.

This is why the Constitution should be expected to govern the platform for decades: it is built to outlive everything below it. The Constitution is the platform's permanent law; everything else is implementation.
