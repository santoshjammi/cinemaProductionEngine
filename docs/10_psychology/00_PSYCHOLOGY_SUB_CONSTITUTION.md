# Psychology Sub-Constitution

**Document ID:** PSC-001  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** CONSTITUTIONAL  
**Initial scope:** Husband-wife relationship and emotional psychology

## Preamble

This niche creates **story-first cinematic entertainment about ordinary relationship and emotional problems**.

The goal is to entertain, help viewers recognize mistakes and patterns, encourage constructive understanding, strengthen the idea of family and responsible relationship behavior, and build a commercially successful media property.

This niche is intentionally **non-clinical**.

---

## Article 1 — Initial domain

Version 1 focuses on:

> **Committed husband-wife relationship and emotional psychology.**

Parenting may appear when the husband-wife dynamic is materially affected by children or family responsibilities.

Child psychology is not automatically part of this constitution.

Future psychology domains require explicit expansion.

---

## Article 2 — Philosophical center

> **Strong relationships are not relationships without problems. They are relationships in which people learn to face problems together.**

And:

> **Love survives when people choose understanding, responsibility, communication, and growth over ego and avoidance.**

The stories may be complicated.

They should not be cynical about committed relationships.

---

## Article 3 — Entertainment, not clinical authority

Required disclaimer principle:

> These stories are fictional dramatizations created for entertainment and reflection. They are not psychological diagnosis, therapy, medical advice, clinical guidance, or scientific assessment. Psychological interpretations portrayed are narrative representations of human behavior and should not be treated as professional evaluation.

The production may discuss ordinary emotions and behavior.

It must not impersonate professional diagnosis.

---

## Article 4 — Excluded territory by default

The initial niche does not center stories on:

- abuse;
- coercive control;
- suicide or self-harm;
- addiction;
- criminal psychology;
- explicit sexual material;
- infidelity sensationalism;
- extreme trauma;
- psychiatric diagnosis;
- pseudo-scientific certainty;
- rage bait;
- relationship paranoia.

If a future product direction wishes to address a materially different domain, it requires constitutional review.

---

## Article 5 — Constructive problem space

Examples of valid territory include:

- emotional withdrawal;
- communication breakdown;
- fear of vulnerability;
- work stress affecting marriage;
- job loss;
- professional failure;
- financial pressure;
- feeling unappreciated;
- insecurity;
- jealousy without infidelity;
- misunderstanding;
- unresolved arguments;
- emotional distance;
- lack of quality time;
- differing expectations;
- household-load imbalance;
- parenting disagreements;
- in-law/family pressure;
- career-versus-family priorities;
- trust after minor dishonesty;
- lack of affection;
- communication-style differences;
- difficulty apologizing;
- difficulty forgiving;
- personal-space needs;
- digital distraction;
- work-from-home boundaries.

The RPCO is the canonical expandable content map.

---

## Article 6 — One primary psychological mechanism per production

Each episode or multi-part arc has one dominant mechanism.

Secondary mechanisms may exist, but they may not compete for central meaning.

### Preferred narrative mechanism model

```text
ordinary trigger / pressure
        ↓
character interpretation
        ↓
emotional response
        ↓
protective intention
        ↓
observable behavior
        ↓
partner interpretation
        ↓
relationship consequence
        ↓
recognition
        ↓
constructive response
```

Do not require clinical labels such as attachment style, disorder, trauma classification, defense mechanism, or cognitive-bias taxonomy.

---

## Article 7 — Story first

The viewer should experience a compelling relationship story before feeling that they are receiving a lesson.

Psychological explanation should normally emerge through:

- behavior;
- dialogue;
- reaction;
- consequence;
- contrast;
- choice.

A brief explicit “beneath the silence” interpretation may be used when it materially helps understanding.

It must not become a lecture.

### Good use

> “What Sarah heard as rejection was, for Mark, an attempt to hide how afraid he was of disappointing her.”

Then return to the story.

### Poor use

A narrator repeatedly naming every emotion and explaining every action while the characters merely illustrate the narration.

---

## Article 8 — Resolution

Stories must move toward:

- complete repair; or
- meaningful progress; or
- a clear constructive next step.

A story may end with unresolved practical complexity.

It may not end in nihilism merely for impact.

When full healing is impossible within the runtime, the conclusion should still serve a greater good.

---

## Article 9 — Human imperfection

Good people may behave poorly.

Allowed examples:

- defensiveness;
- unfair words;
- selfish choices;
- failing to listen;
- embarrassment-based concealment;
- misplaced blame;
- broken promises;
- poor timing;
- temporary work/family imbalance.

Do not require either spouse to be a villain.

---

## Article 10 — Responsibility must vary

Across the catalog:

- sometimes one spouse contributes more;
- sometimes the other;
- often both;
- sometimes neither is wrong;
- sometimes external circumstances create incompatible needs.

A permanent “flawed man / emotionally wiser woman” or inverse pattern is prohibited.

---

## Article 11 — Emotional palette

Relationship cinema should not be permanently melancholic.

Use, when appropriate:

- warmth;
- humor;
- teasing;
- affection;
- ordinary domestic happiness;
- awkwardness;
- tenderness;
- frustration;
- sadness;
- uncertainty;
- relief;
- joy.

Warmth is important because the viewer must understand why the relationship is worth protecting.

---

## Article 12 — Visual language

Default Psychology visual language:

- cinematic realism;
- restrained performance;
- natural interiors;
- controlled camera language;
- believable domestic/work environments;
- meaningful negative space;
- reaction-based coverage;
- visual continuity.

Stylization may vary by format, but emotional legibility and realism should remain.

---

## Article 13 — Formats

The niche supports multiple formats.

Initial launch:

1. 8–15 minute 16:9 cinematic relationship story.
2. 30–90 second derivative Short/Reel from the same approved production.

Future formats may include:

- compact 3–5 minute stories;
- narrated stories;
- “Beneath the Silence” explainers;
- compilations;
- other approved formats.

---

## Article 14 — Content hierarchy

The niche should be planned using:

```text
Psychology
  ↓
Relationship & Emotional Psychology
  ↓
Problem Family
  ↓
Sub-Series / Mechanism Family
  ↓
Mechanism Variant
  ↓
Story Universe
  ↓
Story Arc
  ↓
Episode / Part
```

Example:

```text
Psychology
→ Relationship & Emotional Psychology
→ Emotional Withdrawal
→ Fear-Based Withdrawal
→ fear of disappointing spouse after job loss
→ Mark & Sarah
→ job-loss withdrawal and reconnection arc
→ Episode
```

---

## Article 15 — Sub-series admission

A sub-series exists only when it has a materially distinct:

- trigger;
- mechanism;
- recurring viewer question;
- relationship dynamic;
- story pattern; or
- resolution path.

Do not manufacture categories merely to create taxonomy volume.

---

## Article 16 — Multi-part stories

Multi-part stories are allowed.

Before Part 1 is produced, GENESIS must know:

- full causal arc;
- final emotional direction;
- final logical or emotional resolution;
- part boundaries;
- what each part changes.

Commercial performance may influence presentation, not the already-approved moral/causal destination.

---

## Article 17 — Soul and business

The channel is intended to make significant money.

Its deeper purpose is also to leave viewers with something that can improve how people understand themselves, their spouse, family, and responsibility toward society.

Commercial ambition should fund and scale meaningful storytelling rather than degrade it.
