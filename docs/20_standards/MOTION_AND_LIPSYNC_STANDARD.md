# Motion & Lip-Sync Standard

**Version:** 1.0

## Principle

Visible speech should look performed.

But not every frame needs lip sync.

Use cinematic coverage to place effort where viewers can judge it.

## Shot rules

### Speaker close/medium
If the speaking mouth is clearly readable:

**production-approved lip sync is mandatory.**

### Listener reaction
No lip sync required.

Use facial/body reaction.

### Over-the-shoulder
Lip sync required when the speaking face is readable.

### Wide two-shot
Lip sync requirement may be relaxed when mouths are too small to judge, but body timing must still feel plausible.

### Insert/cutaway
Dialogue may continue off-screen without lip sync.

### Voiceover
Allowed only when the scene/format explicitly calls for it.

Voiceover must not be introduced merely to hide failed facial animation.

---

## Anti-static-dialogue rule

A long dialogue scene cannot be realized as a single static image with TTS and pan/zoom.

At minimum, use meaningful:

- shot changes;
- reactions;
- body movement;
- eyeline shifts;
- domestic actions;
- speaker/listener coverage.

---

## Performance continuity

Motion should match:

- emotion;
- dialogue pace;
- physical geography;
- previous shot;
- next shot.

Avoid random gesturing.

---

## Failure examples

- visible mouth completely still during a close speaking shot;
- lip sync far enough off to distract;
- both characters moving lips when only one speaks;
- character gaze changes arbitrarily;
- repeated canned nodding;
- motion inserted only because the model generated it.
